# Room-Temperature Saltwater Food Forest — Spec + Build Plan

**Captured 2026-07-09 from Dustin's notes (existed nowhere else — this
file is the canonical record).** Priority: build AFTER the household
nutrition goal (`HOUSEHOLD_NUTRITION_PLAN.md`) and the hydroponic
garden. This system supplies the nutrients hydroponics CANNOT —
**iodine, sodium, chloride** (Dulse/Kelp/Sea Lettuce/Red Ogo) — plus
protein diversity (scallops, mussels, anchovies, sardines) and wax-
production feedstock. That cross-link is why it closes the household
nutrition ledger.

## Goal
A saltwater tank that operates at ROOM TEMPERATURE, is largely self-
maintaining + self-cleaning via its inhabitants, and forms a roughly
circular ecosystem. Ideally every organism is harvestable for human food
AND/OR wax production (candles, lost-wax, sculpting). Modular: 30-gallon
tanks grown iteratively into a larger interconnected system —
reproducible + scalable for households and small businesses.

## Design rules
- Start with a single 30-gallon tank; introduce life gradually as the
  environment suits each role.
- Tank connectors ≥ 3 inches diameter (sardine/anchovy schools need room).
- Anchovies/Sardines only after ≥ 6 interconnected tanks. Add them when
  adding tanks 5 & 6 together: tank 5 = wider, open swimming, fewer
  plants; tank 6 = plentiful rocks for zooplankton + fish hiding.
- Between plankton and normal tanks (and on algae pipes): a **biochar-
  sand filter** (later: grog+biochar+ceramic core, below) — lets
  nutrients flow through the food forest but blocks larger organic
  cells/particles.
- Plankton/phytoplankton reactors: commercial ones exist but need more
  maintenance; the proposed 3D-printed versions target minimal-
  maintenance, no replaceable filters.
- The primary 6- or 8-tank system needs NO plankton/algae parts and can
  run on a nutrient monitor + manual maintenance with advisory. Plankton
  + algae add-ons REDUCE long-term manual maintenance.

## Organism roster (all considered for the system)
Sea Lettuce, Red Ogo, Scallops, Mussels, Dulse, Tiger Tail Sea
Cucumbers, Trochus Snails, Cerith Snails, Anchovies, Sardines, Chlorella
Salina, Rhodophyta. By ROLE:

- **Room-temp macroalgae (food + wax)**: Sea Lettuce (edible, wax), Red
  Ogo (edible, wax), Chlorella Salina (edible high-starch powder, rice/
  pasta substitute), Rhodophyta (edible Floridian starch powder).
- **Filter feeders (consume fine debris/organic waste → less mechanical
  filtration)**: Scallops (protein), Mussels (protein).
- **Nutrient regulators (invasive-algae prevention)**: Sea Lettuce,
  Palmaria Palmata / Dulse (soup/seasoning/snack additive; polysaccharides
  for wax additive).
- **Nuisance-algae consumers**: Tiger Tail Sea Cucumbers, Mussels, Scallops.
- **Detritus eaters**: Tiger Tail Sea Cucumber, Cerith Snails.
- **Glass cleaners**: Trochus Snails, Cerith Snails.
- **Nutrient replenishers (iron, phosphorus, nitrates)**: Anchovies,
  Sardines (edible, oil-additive production).
- **Substrate oxygenation** (prevent upper-substrate anoxia/smell while
  keeping a deeper anaerobic layer for decomposition→nutrient
  replenishment): Ulva Prolifera (rooting Sea Lettuce; some species
  oxygenate deeper).
- **Good smell**: Codium (oceanic smell just by growing; dried+powdered
  in a filter = air freshener). Codium + Red Ogo + Dulse + Rhodophyta
  powder → stronger air freshener between the enclosed system and the
  outside (on the filter, or spread on the open-top screen cover).

## Build phases (household saltwater food forest)
1. **Foundation (1× 30 gal)** — stable nutrient-balanced environment.
   Filtration/circulation/full-spectrum light (12 h cycle), live rock +
   sand. Add Sea Lettuce, Codium, Red Ogo (first nutrient regulation +
   food). Target ~5–10 ppm nitrate, ~0.02–0.05 ppm phosphate. Cleanup
   crew: Trochus + Cerith snails, gradual. Seed phytoplankton
   (Nannochloropsis / Tetraselmis), dose periodically. **Phase 1.5
   (optional)**: 5-gal phytoplankton refuge.
2. **Diversity (2nd tank)** — 3-inch pipe, gentle flow; plankton +
   detritus zone. Add Tiger Tail Sea Cucumbers + more Cerith. Cultivate
   Dulse (nutrients + food + wax additive). Add zooplankton (copepods,
   rotifers). **Phase 2.5**: 5-gal zooplankton refuge.
3. **Filter feeders + plankton (3rd tank)** — filter-feeder zone, ample
   flow. Add Scallops + Mussels. ≥1 phytoplankton breeding station/
   refugium. Monitor nitrate/phosphate; adjust macroalgae harvest/dosing.
4. **Scaling macroalgae (4th tank)** — strong light, low flow. Add
   Rhodophyta + Chlorella Salina (starch powder). Harvest regularly for
   balance + products. More Trochus + Sea Cucumbers as needed.
5. **Sardines/Anchovies prep (5th + 6th tanks)** — tank 5 open swimming
   (minimal decor), tank 6 hiding/breeding (live rock, caves, biofilm).
   Ensure robust phyto+zooplankton (increase dosing / add a reactor).
   Introduce anchovy/sardine schools; monitor health. Fish waste raises
   nitrate/phosphate → adjust macroalgae harvest + nutrient exports.
6. **Optimization + maintenance** — regular harvesting (macroalgae for
   food/wax; rotate scallops/mussels; sustainable fish harvest). Test
   nitrate/phosphate/salinity/trace. Should reach near-self-sustaining.
7. **8-tank expansion** — 2 more starch-producing tanks (like phase 4) +
   Pacific White Shrimp + Spirulina (protein variety + spirulina-noodle
   potential).

## CO2 numbers (Dustin's estimates — flag as priors)
- Default 8-tank system ≈ **1.8 kg CO2 cycling / year**.
- With denser macroalgae + larger (5–10 gal) plankton reactors →
  **3–4 kg CO2 sequestration/cycling per year** per 8-tank system.
- ⇒ ~2–3 8-tank systems per household to offset household CO2 (if using
  only these).

## Modular Filter Core (grog + biochar + porous ceramic)
Centralized, tier-scalable, low-cost, long-lived, locally maintainable.
- **Grog** = powdered ceramics / fire-ceramics (fire clay or buy cheap).
  Primary filter — blocks essentially all bio-material. Near-indefinite
  life; renew by soaking in **white vinegar** to clear mineral buildup.
- **Biochar** = intermediate filter; limits nutrient passthrough so
  algae don't strip the main tanks via uncontrolled growth. Biochar
  thickness sets nutrient passthrough.
- **Porous ceramic** enclosure/separators: pore size < 1 mm (ideally
  ~0.5 mm), smaller than the media they contain. Make by fine-grinding
  Rosin/Tallow/Pitch, embedding in the clay, firing as a disk to fit the
  container.
- **Symmetric bidirectional layer stack** (usable both ways for
  cleaning/backflow):
  1. Full nutrient + biomass blocking:
     `Ceramic 0.5mm (5–10mm) · Fine Grog 1–3mm (10–20mm) · Coarse Grog
     5–10mm (20–30mm) · Ceramic (5–10mm) · BioChar (20–50mm) · Ceramic
     (5–10mm) · Coarse Grog (20–30mm) · Fine Grog (10–20mm) · Ceramic
     (5–10mm)`.
  2. Full biomass + PARTIAL nutrient blocking: reduce biochar to a
     10–20mm gradient, alternate layers (tune to desired flow/control).
  3. Full biomass block, nutrient NON-blocking: remove biochar + the
     middle two ceramic sections → grog+ceramic only (unrestricted
     nutrient flow, blocks algae/plankton/waste).
- A single electric pump + alternator periodically switches between
  filter types for controlled nutrient filtration; apply periodic
  backflow to keep grog+biochar effective.
- **Core device**: 8 inlet + 8 outlet pipes (custom disk filters; expand
  one electronic filter across multiple algae pipes / plankton reactors;
  start with just the disk you need). Flow driven by an electric or
  magnetic field, modulated to focus one channel or spread across all 8;
  field generators at BOTH top and bottom; power-modulated for finer
  control. 8 insertable pipe SIZES (fully utilized = 16 pipes, 2 of each
  size). Each disk has equal input/output pipes on opposite sides.
- Algae pipes are the final custom add-on (not commercially available;
  needs advanced filtering). Add plankton/phytoplankton + algae pipes
  carefully to smaller test systems first (limited data so far).
