What Polari is

Polari is the connective substrate for a family of open-source projects. Each is useful on its own — but together they form a working baseline: the knowledge, tooling, and infrastructure a community needs to build things, make decisions in the open, keep its network alive, and sustain itself without depending on closed platforms. This site is the front door — it links out to the full apps and hosts the docs, so the heavier tools stay free for the people doing serious work.

PRF

Polari Research Framework

No-code research & simulation

A multi-node environment where research becomes configurable objects: agroforestry and hydroponics, materials science, multi-scale simulation across FEM, DFT and molecular dynamics, and FPGA and hardware workflows — built without writing code.

Open the framework → DPS

Democratic Political Scorecard

Evidence-first political scoring

Score policy, legislation, judicial decisions, and groups on the issues you actually care about — with every score traceable back to its sources, and the scoring rules kept in the open rather than behind a curtain.

Isle-Mesh

Local-first mesh networking

Resilient networking that keeps nodes connected and useful without leaning on centralized infrastructure — the fabric the rest of Polari can run across when the wider internet is slow, costly, or gone.

OSEB

Open Source Economic Baseline

The map that indexes the rest

A generalizable tech tree of the knowledge and tools an open economy needs — where each node isn't just a description but a working, runnable module. It's the index the other three projects plug into.

Open the baseline →
The through-line

Every part of Polari holds to the same commitments: no-code where it can be, so people who aren't engineers can still build and decide; evidence you can trace, so claims and scores hold up to scrutiny; local-first, so the tools work close to the ground; and open source, end to end, so the baseline belongs to everyone who uses it.
